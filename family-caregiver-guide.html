Family caregiver guide

Share the load without becoming the family switchboard.

Family caregivers often inherit the whole operation overnight: medications, rides, neighbors, and a sibling who wants updates but cannot take Thursday. This guide is for the first weeks — when a system is kinder than heroics.

Updated September 2026 · 9 min read

Name a coordinator, then refuse to be the only one

Someone has to own invitations and the week. That person should not also be the only visitor, the only driver, and the only one who knows the pharmacy hours. Write the split down where the circle can see it. Informal “we’ll figure it out” is how the same daughter ends up on every night.

Famielda for families is built for this role: you coordinate the circle even when you cannot be in the room.

The first seven days

  1. Create one senior profile: contacts, routines, and what a new helper must not have to guess.
  2. Invite the people already helping — relatives first, then caregivers if they are already in the home.
  3. Put this week on a shared schedule, including the gaps. Empty afternoons are information.
  4. Agree how urgent news travels. A governed feed beats a 40-message thread at 11 p.m.

If the conversation with a parent is still ahead of you, start with how to talk about accepting help.

Siblings, distance, and the myth of equal hours

Equal hours are rare. Equal visibility is possible. The sibling in another city can own documents, appointment reminders, or weekend coverage without pretending they can take the Tuesday bath. Long-distance family caregivers need the same record as the person down the street. See care from a distance.

Protect the coordinator

Burnout looks like skipped meals and a calendar that only works if you cancel your own life. Build a backup before you need one. A neighbor check-in, a professional visit, or a second family member with schedule access is a plan. A promise to “text if you need me” is not.

Common questions

What should a family caregiver do in the first week?

Write down who owns invitations and the week, gather contacts and medications into one profile, and invite the people already helping — including at least one backup for days you cannot cover.

How do siblings share caregiving without fighting?

Give each person a visible role and a slice of the week instead of forwarding every update in a group chat. The record should show who is covering, not who texted last.

When is a family caregiver stretched too thin?

Recurring missed visits, one person making every decision at midnight, or no backup for a work day are signs to invite more family or professional caregivers into the same circle.

This family caregiver guide is for coordination, not clinical advice. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
